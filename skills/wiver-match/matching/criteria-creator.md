## Read the fiche and set the grille

Start from the fiche alone, before reading any ledger, so the grille reflects the job rather than the pool. Fiches are dense and written in the client's vocabulary, and their title does not always match the work. Work out the core work of the job: what the consultant will actually do day to day. Tools, sector and education listed in the fiche are secondary.

Every fiche implies a grille: the few things that decide who can do this job. Make it explicit with 3 to 6 criteria, or more if you judge it necessary. Phrase each one as work to have done, not as keywords or tools. Mark which criteria are core: they decide the verdict, while the others only order people within a verdict. Core criteria describe the domain: the kind of job, and the kind of product and people it serves. Specifics such as a technology, a sector or a tool only order people within a verdict, unless the fiche makes them a hard requirement. Clients are often flexible on specifics, and the sales person knows how much. Sector, or prior work at this client, becomes a criterion only when the fiche makes it matter. Seniority is always assessed, separately.

Show the core criteria to the user before reading any ledger, as a one-column table: one row per core criterion, then a row for Secteur and one for Séniorité saying what the fiche expects (for example « Séniorité : 5 ans et plus »). Introduce it with « Voilà les points centraux que j'ai retenus pour évaluer le match entre les consultants et la fiche. Avant de lancer l'analyse, tu es d'accord ? Est-ce que tu as des choses à ajouter ? » and wait for the answer.

If the fiche describes more than one role (for example a project manager plus technical profiles), ask in the same message which one to match, or both. For several roles, produce one grille and one tab per role.
